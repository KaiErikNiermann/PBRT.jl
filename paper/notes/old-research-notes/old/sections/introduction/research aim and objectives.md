## Refined Draft
The primary aim of this work to present a large structural overview of the modern state of software interop, and to link and contrast various concepts from this with an applied practical example. The goal of this being to create somewhat comprehensive overview of the world of language interoperability while providing a concrete example which applies some modern approaches. Similarly to various other fields in computer science interoperability nowadays forms a rather expansive collection of research and real world/industry application. While this is of course broadly speaking positive I believe there are insights to be gained from creating a more unified albeit constrained picture of the larger field. Generally speaking its not too uncommon that you can see a dichotomy form in different aspects of computer science between what works and has worked for a while; often taking the form of legacy systems which are iteratively patched to be good enough; and newer research which can often be somewhat inaccessable, complex to adapt, and difficult to see the real utility of. The same can indeed be seen for the case of software interoperability, thus with this work I hope to myself gain but also provide some insights into how some of these newer developments in the design of programming languages, interoperability layers and polyglot systems can potentially assist in creating more effective software solutions.
	
To put it more concretely, there are several objectives I aim to achieve with this work. Firstly, I intend to research and present an overview of the modern state of language interoperability, with a focus on the more recent developments in the field. My goal is to examine how various aspects of language interoperability have evolved over time, thus presenting a more unified understanding of the domain. By gathering comprehensive insights into the current state of language interoperability, I plan to compare and contrast these findings with an applied example. This analysis will extend to assessing the benefits and drawbacks of employing modern techniques, particularly focusing on language paradigms within interoperability, the performance dynamics in critical systems, and the influence of modern type systems on the design and application of interoperability frameworks. Overall, this work aims to aggregate and convey a broad understanding of the modern state of software interoperability, linking theoretical concepts to practical examples, and critically assessing both the advantages and limitations as well as identifying areas for future exploration. The ultimate hope is to provide meaningful insights that could serve as a foundation for further research in this intriguing area of study.
