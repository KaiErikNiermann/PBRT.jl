\section{Conclusion}\label{s:conclusion}

Understanding the modern state of language interoperability is a complex task. Everything, from the lowest level of the programming stack to the highest level of abstraction, plays a role in how well languages interact with one another. Despite this complexity, a structure exists that can be understood and further utilized to comprehend better how interoperability tools have evolved and are likely to continue evolving. The goal of this paper was to develop a framework for understanding the evolution of more traditionally based interoperability tools and to build upon this framework, along with an existing tool, to explore more complex, niche, and potential future applications and assess their broader viability. The primary insight of this paper is that FFI-based tools, despite their long history, remain highly relevant and have considerable room for further evolution in the future. A secondary insight is that FFI and more unified execution models, along with their associated interoperability tools, are not entirely mutually exclusive. This means that many of the more recent theoretical foundations of unified execution models can be applied to FFI-based tools to enhance their performance and usability. Nonetheless, it's ultimately challenging to predict how things will evolve in the future; however, it's pretty evident that language interoperability is a complex and multifaceted problem deeply tied to the evolution of programming languages.
